# Explainable AI for Student Academic Success Prediction and Early Risk Detection

## Abstract

The increasing availability of educational data from Learning Management Systems (LMS) has made student academic success prediction and early risk detection prominent areas of research. This systematic literature review explores the application of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), machine learning, and data mining in education and learning analytics for predicting student academic behavior and identifying at-risk students.

Following a PRISMA-based screening process, **45 studies published between 2021 and 2026** were analyzed based on three research questions addressing:

1. The prominent behavioral factors associated with student academic performance.
2. The predictive and explainable techniques used for academic risk detection.
3. The effectiveness of XAI-based early warning systems.

The findings demonstrate that **student engagement, attendance, assignment completion, self-regulated learning, and LMS participation frequency** are among the most significant factors influencing academic performance.

Among the predictive techniques, **Random Forest, XGBoost, Decision Trees, and Neural Networks** were identified as the most widely used machine learning models. For explainability, **SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME (Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations)** were the most commonly applied techniques.

Although previous research demonstrates strong predictive performance and improved model interpretability, practical and scalable early warning systems remain insufficiently developed. This review highlights current trends, key challenges, and future directions for developing **user-centric, explainable, and intervention-oriented educational analytics systems**.

Such systems can provide valuable insights for researchers, educators, and educational institutions seeking to support student success through transparent and data-driven decision-making.

## Research Focus

This systematic literature review focuses on three major areas:

### 1. Student Academic Behaviour

Identifying the behavioral factors that have the greatest influence on student academic performance, including:

- Student engagement
- Attendance
- Assignment completion
- Self-regulated learning
- LMS participation frequency

### 2. Predictive and Explainable Techniques

Examining the machine learning and explainable AI techniques used to predict academic performance and identify at-risk students.

Common predictive models include:

- Random Forest
- XGBoost
- Decision Trees
- Neural Networks

Common XAI techniques include:

- SHAP
- LIME

### 3. XAI-Based Early Warning Systems

Evaluating how explainable AI can support early warning systems by making predictions more understandable to educators and other stakeholders.

## Research Gap

Despite the strong predictive performance reported in existing studies, several gaps remain:

- Limited development of practical early warning systems.
- Lack of scalable solutions for real-world educational environments.
- Insufficient integration of prediction and actionable intervention.
- Need for more user-centric and transparent educational analytics.
- Limited focus on translating model predictions into meaningful interventions for educators and students.

---

## Conclusion

The review demonstrates that combining **machine learning, learning analytics, and Explainable AI** can provide valuable support for predicting student academic performance and identifying students who may be at risk.

However, future research should move beyond prediction alone and focus on developing **transparent, scalable, user-centric, and intervention-oriented early warning systems** that can help educators make informed decisions and provide timely support to students.
